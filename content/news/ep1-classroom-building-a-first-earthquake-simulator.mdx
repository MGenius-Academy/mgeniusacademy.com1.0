The EP1 classroom engaged students in hands-on exploration by challenging them to construct their own earthquake simulator. The project addressed fundamental questions: What is an earthquake? Why do buildings shake or collapse? How can structures become more stable?

![EP1 earthquake simulator project](/images/post_ep1-classroom-building-a-first-earthquake-simulator/image-10.png)

## Construction & Programming

Students built a platform using **simple materials such as straws, connectors, and cardboard** to simulate seismic movement. They then employed Micro:bit programming to create different levels and patterns of vibration. Small buildings were constructed atop the platform to observe structural responses during simulated earthquakes.

![Students building the earthquake simulator platform](/images/post_ep1-classroom-building-a-first-earthquake-simulator/image-30-1024x768.jpeg)

## Key Observations

Testing revealed significant differences in structural performance — some buildings "collapsed very quickly, while others remained surprisingly stable." This prompted deeper investigation into the causes of these variations.

![Testing structures on the simulator](/images/post_ep1-classroom-building-a-first-earthquake-simulator/image-25-1024x768.jpeg)

## Design Improvements

Students refined their designs through iterative processes by:

- Reinforcing structures
- Changing connection methods
- Adjusting the center of gravity
- Adding additional support

![Students refining their building designs](/images/post_ep1-classroom-building-a-first-earthquake-simulator/image-29-1024x768.jpeg)

## Core Philosophy

Through experimentation and redesign cycles, students discovered that:

> "Stability is not based on luck — it is the result of thoughtful design and structural engineering."

MGA emphasizes that learning involves identifying problems and discovering solutions through experimentation rather than simply obtaining correct answers initially.
