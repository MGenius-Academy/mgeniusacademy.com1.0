MGA's EP4 classroom introduces students to electronic engineering through building a knob-controlled desk lamp. The instruction emphasizes foundational questioning — "Why does a light turn on?" — and explores electrical circuit principles systematically.

![Students in the EP4 classroom exploring electronic engineering](/images/post_ep4-classroom-designing-a-knob-controlled-lamp-and-discovering-how-electricity-works/image-47-1024x768.jpeg)

## Circuit Learning

Students progress through understanding:

- How electricity flows through circuits
- Why certain connections illuminate the LED Light Panel while others fail
- Brightness control mechanisms

![Circuit connection and LED panel work](/images/post_ep4-classroom-designing-a-knob-controlled-lamp-and-discovering-how-electricity-works/image-62-1024x768.jpeg)

## Brightness Control Concept

Students advance to asking:

> "What if I don't just want the light to turn on — what if I want to adjust the brightness?"

This leads to learning about potentiometers. Key concepts taught include:

- Switches control on/off functionality
- Driver boards and potentiometers adjust brightness through resistance control

![Potentiometer and circuit assembly](/images/post_ep4-classroom-designing-a-knob-controlled-lamp-and-discovering-how-electricity-works/image-53-1024x768.jpeg)

## Troubleshooting and Problem-Solving

Students encounter challenges requiring diagnostic thinking:

- Lights failing to activate
- Knobs not adjusting brightness correctly
- Questions about connection errors, solder joint stability, and current direction

MGA emphasizes that real engineering is never just about following instructions, but rather involves discovering problems, questioning, and solving them through exploration and experimentation.
