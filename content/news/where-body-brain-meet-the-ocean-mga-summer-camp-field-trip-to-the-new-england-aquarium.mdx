## Different Bodies, Different Adaptations

At the Giant Ocean Tank, students observed fish at various ocean depths and explored questions including:

- Why do some fish stay near the surface while others prefer deeper water?
- Why are some fish incredibly fast, while others rely on camouflage to hide?

Through presentations with aquarium educators, "every animal's body is specially adapted to its environment." Students learned that marine animals have evolved distinctive features enabling survival in ocean conditions, similar to how human bodies enable running, jumping, and thinking.

## Becoming Marine Scientists

Interactive activities led by aquarium education staff engaged students with card games exploring how "salinity, pH, and water temperature" affect marine ecosystems. Students discovered that minor environmental changes influence animal growth, food-finding, and survival, emphasizing ocean protection importance.

## Discovering the Ocean Through Touch

The Touch Tank proved most popular, allowing students to gently touch sharks and rays. Student inquiries included: "*Why doesn't a shark feel the way I expected?*" and "*Why is a ray so soft?*"

Students learned that "patience and careful observation were just as important as curiosity," strengthening focus and scientific observation skills through waiting and observing.
