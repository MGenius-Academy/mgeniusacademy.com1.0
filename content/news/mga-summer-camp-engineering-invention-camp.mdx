## More Than Crafts — Becoming Young Engineers

The camp emphasized hands-on engineering rather than following instructions. Campers "imagined, designed, tested, improved, and created—transforming ordinary materials into inventions of their own." Throughout the week, students explored mechanical structures, circuits, levers, sound, and engineering design.

![Students beginning the Engineering Invention Camp](/images/post_mga-summer-camp-engineering-invention-camp/IMG_1233-1024x768.jpg)

## Building the First Invention

Students began by constructing cardboard cars, learning to use cutting tools safely and build sturdy structures. Many students expanded beyond the basic design—some added robotic features like arms and faces. Older campers took it further by asking, "What if we added a motor to make the robot's head turn?" This experimentation resulted in rotating-head robots, demonstrating that "engineering rarely has only one right answer."

## Discovering Mechanical Principles

Using popsicle sticks and rubber bands, campers built catapults while exploring levers and mechanical advantage. Afternoon activities included designing Rube Goldberg machines as teams, requiring "careful planning, teamwork, and repeated testing."

## Designing Music

Before building cardboard guitars, campers investigated how sound is created through vibration. Students discovered that different rubber band types and thicknesses produced different pitches, leading them to experiment with various materials as guitar strings.
