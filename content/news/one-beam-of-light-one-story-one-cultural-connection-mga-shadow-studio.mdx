## From Audience to Director

The camp began with a live shadow puppet performance by an instructor, inviting students to think like storytellers. Discussion prompts included: "What kinds of theater have you seen? What makes a performance memorable?"

Each child then built a personal shadow theater. Some designed movable stage backdrops, others created puppet storage systems, and a few invented "security cameras" with googly eyes to monitor the audience and backstage crew. Every theater reflected its creator's unique imagination.

## Discovering the Engineering Behind a Puppet

Students learned that creating beautiful puppets required them to move naturally. Starting with simple one-rod puppets, they advanced to articulated puppets controlled by two rods. Through testing and revision, students discovered that movable joints must be separated and connected individually for realistic motion.

They experimented, observed, redesigned, and improved—thinking like real engineers throughout the process.

## When the Classroom Became an Optical Laboratory

Students investigated the history of traditional shadow puppetry while experimenting with different transparent materials. They tested whether colored markers could create vivid projected colors, what happened when two colored transparent sheets were layered together, and how different color combinations produced new effects.

Students repeatedly tested materials under light, adjusted designs, and projected them onto screens repeatedly to discover how transparency, color layering, and lighting affect stage appearance.

## Inspiration from Two Very Different Museums

**Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum:** Students explored how museums tell stories and how exhibitions, spaces, and personal collections shape the way stories are shared.

**Museum of Science:** Students investigated reflection, refraction, and light behavior. Some created stories inspired by galaxies after visiting "Cosmic Light," while others developed adventures featuring animals from the "Natural Mysteries" exhibit. Each student chose one exhibit that inspired them and created an original story.

## Bringing Science Onto the Stage

As performance day approached, students refined puppets and theaters. Some used lasers and transparent tape to create dramatic lightning effects. Others modified UFO models to change light transmission. Many adjusted lighting positions repeatedly for perfect shadow projection.

Students applied concepts such as reflection, blocking, diffusion, and light transmission as they improved productions through natural learning via creating, experimenting, and problem-solving.

## Every Child Had a Chance to Shine

On the final day, the classroom transformed into a real theater. Students completed voice recordings, rehearsed together, arranged the stage, and assigned seating for parents before presenting their original shadow puppet performances.

Over the course of the camp, they experienced:

- Engineering through continual design improvement
- Science through questioning and testing ideas
- Artistry and storytelling through performance

## A Heartwarming Parent Reflection

> "This was kid's first visit to the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum. She came home excited to tell me everything she learned about the museum and its stories."

The parent noted their child became fascinated by shadow puppet performances and subsequently watched documentaries to learn more during breaks.

## Why Do We Continue Sharing Shadow Puppetry?

This summer's two Shadow Studio camps marked **Ms. Shirley's 50th community outreach performance**. When asked why she continues, her response is:

> "We hope children can build a meaningful connection with their own culture. We hope one day they will feel proud of where they come from. We hope these small experiences help them become more confident, more courageous, and more willing to express themselves."

## The Story Doesn't End Here…

Several campers will bring their original shadow puppet performances to the community through public performances at:

- Boston Children's Museum
- Wellesley Library
- Brookline Village Library
- Coolidge Corner Library
